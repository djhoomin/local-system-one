How the 19 ms model was made

  
Nothing under 3B parameters could do this zero-shot — the small models all failed through some systematic bias, never noise. So the fast model had to be trained, and the recipe is just distillation from calibrated targets.

  

    
teachers
Two local LLMs label 2,300 synthetic states
Gemma 3 12B routes the tool; Gemma 3n E4B scores urgency and the yes/no checks. Answers are read off first-token logprobs, one prefill per question, never a generation.

    
calibration
Their probabilities get fixed first
Raw LLM logprobs are absurdly overconfident (0.99 on everything). One fitted temperature per head, from 123 hand-labeled states, turns them into targets worth copying.

    
student
ModernBERT-base learns the distribution
Four linear heads on a 149M encoder, trained on the soft targets for 8 minutes. It comes out calibrated by construction: fitting a temperature to its output afterwards gives T ≈ 0.9 — nothing to fix.

  






  
What it costs and what you get

  
Measured on 123 human-labeled states the student never saw, on an M1 Pro. The 12B is the local accuracy ceiling; the student gets 92% of it at 1/290th the latency. Laya and Von are the open general encoders that appeared the same week — the question schema is an input, like Jev — and on an unseen schema they route at 0.33 and 0.45. The interface is free; the general behaviour isn't.
